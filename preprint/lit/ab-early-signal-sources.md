## Five lines: what the evidence shows and does not show
1. Some non-hospital data (rail, 25,000 workplaces, a Japanese cohort) give qualified support that near misses and harm share causes (items 1, 4, 5).
2. I found no hospital study in this search that shows a near-miss rate predicts later patient harm.
3. National hospital data suggest reporting rates measure reporting culture, and do not track harm or mortality (items 6, 7).
4. Most medication errors do not become harm, so the pyramid ratio is weak and varies by how severity is defined (items 4, 10).
5. A score that weights harm from near-miss and no-harm reports should be framed as a learning and signal tool, not a validated predictor of harm.
