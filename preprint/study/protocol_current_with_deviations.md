## A. Aims

1. **Calibration (primary):** measure the in-control false-alarm behaviour of the method **as shipped** (issues 1, 4, 5, 8) at its settings k = 2 (dashboard default "Standard (2σ)") and k = 3.
2. **Detection:** compare how fast the as-shipped method, a revised method and standard charts detect a true rise in harm, at a **common in-control ARL of 365 days** (one false alarm per ward-year).
3. **Confounding:** measure how often each method signals when only *reporting* rises and harm does not (issue 2).
4. **Severity:** measure detection of a shift in severity mix at constant volume, where frequency-only charts are blind (issue 3).
5. **Weights:** measure how much the results depend on the A-I weight scheme (issue 3).

Not an aim: estimating true incidence. The score measures *reported* harm burden (A1-A3).

## D. Data-generating mechanisms

One replicate = one ward, one stream of daily incident reports.

- **Timeline:** Phase I baseline days 1-120, then monitoring days 121-485 (365 days). In-control scenarios monitor for 1,095 days (3 years) to estimate ARL₀ with less censoring. Change point τ = day 181 (60 days into monitoring) for out-of-control scenarios.
- **Daily count:** $N_t \sim \text{NB}(\text{mean } \mu_t \rho_t,\ \text{size } \theta)$; $\theta = \infty$ means Poisson. $\mu_t$ = true event rate per day, $\rho_t$ = reporting propensity (1 at baseline).
- **Severity:** each report draws a level A-I from $\pi_t$. Baseline $\pi_0$ = (0.20, 0.25, 0.25, 0.15, 0.08, 0.04, 0.02, 0.007, 0.003), the step 3 mix (about 70% A-C, 1% G-I).
- **Daily score:** $x_t = \sum w(\text{level})$ over the day's reports; 0 on days with none.
- **Exposure:** constant (one ward, fixed beds), so rate and count charts coincide; an exposure-varying scenario is out of scope (§8).

### Scenarios (18)

Core: μ₀ = 2/day, θ = 2.

| ID | Type | Change at τ | μ₀ | θ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IC1-IC9 | In control | none | 0.5, 2, 8 (each) | 1, 2, ∞ (each), full 3 × 3 |
| S15 | Step in harm rate | μ × 1.5 | 2 | 2 |
| S2 | Step in harm rate | μ × 2 | 2 | 2 |
| S2-small | Step | μ × 2 | 0.5 | 2 |
| S2-large | Step | μ × 2 | 8 | 2 |
| S2-θ1 | Step | μ × 2 | 2 | 1 |
| S2-Pois | Step | μ × 2 | 2 | ∞ |
| R60 | Linear ramp | μ rises linearly from μ₀ to 2μ₀ over days τ to τ+59, then stays at 2μ₀ | 2 | 2 |
| SEV | Severity-mix shift, constant volume | share of E-I doubled (from 15% to 30%), A-D rescaled to keep the total at 1 | 2 | 2 |
| REP | Reporting-rate shift, constant harm | ρ × 1.5 for levels A-D only (more near-miss/no-harm reporting, e.g. a campaign or new system); E-I unchanged | 2 | 2 |

REP is generated by thinning/adding at the level of each category: A-D reports have mean μ₀ρπ₀(A-D), E-I reports keep μ₀π₀(E-I). Detection in REP is a **false signal about harm**: lower is better.

## E. Estimands and targets

For each method m and scenario:
- **ARL₀** (in control): expected days from the start of monitoring to the first alarm.
- **FA365:** P(at least one alarm within 365 monitoring days) in control.
- **Alarm-days per ward-year** and **alarm episodes per ward-year** (an episode = consecutive alarm days, issue 7).
- **CED** (conditional expected delay): E[T - τ | T ≥ τ], with T the first alarm day; reported with the share of replicates censored at the end of monitoring.
- **POD14, POD30:** P(τ ≤ T ≤ τ + 13) and P(τ ≤ T ≤ τ + 29), among replicates with no alarm before τ.
- **Pre-τ false alarm:** P(T < τ) in out-of-control scenarios (sanity check against FA365).
- **Endpoint revision** (as-shipped smooth only, issue 6): mean |s_t(data to t) - s_t(data to t+7)| divided by the in-control mean score.

## M. Methods compared

All methods see the same streams (common random numbers). Comparators **M3-M7** use the Phase I days 1-120 as a fixed baseline and are **calibrated to ARL₀ = 365** (§M.2). M1 is run exactly as shipped, uncalibrated, because its calibration is what Aim 1 measures.

| ID | Method | Monitored statistic | Alarm |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1a | **As shipped, k = 2** (`risk_kinematics` v0.1.0, window 7) | engine re-run each day on all data up to that day (calendar from first to last report, mean/SD over all days, centred smooth, 6-day trend rule) | primary: OUTSIDE; secondary: any alert (OUTSIDE or MARGINAL) |
| M1b | As shipped, k = 3 | same | same |
| M2 | **Revised method** (audit fixes 1, 4, 6, 8) | daily score; calendar extended to the analysis day; trailing 7-day mean for display; limit from Phase I | $x_t$ > the (1 - 1/365) quantile of the Phase I fitted compound distribution (NB counts fitted by moments, θ̂ capped at 1000, severity mix π̂ from Phase I with add-one smoothing; quantile from 200,000 simulated days). Trend rule dropped |
| M3 | Shewhart individuals (XmR) on $x_t$ | $x_t$ | $x_t > \bar x_I + L\,\overline{MR}_I/1.128$ (C3) |
| M4 | EWMA on $x_t$, λ = 0.2 | $z_t = \lambda x_t + (1-\lambda) z_{t-1}$, $z_{120} = \bar x_I$ | $z_t > \bar x_I + L\,\hat\sigma_I\sqrt{\lambda/(2-\lambda)}$ (asymptotic limits) (C13, C14) |
| M5 | Upper CUSUM on $x_t$ | $C_t = \max(0, C_{t-1} + x_t - \bar x_I - 0.5\hat\sigma_I)$ | $C_t > h$ (C11) |
| M6 | g-chart on severe events | days between successive E-I reports | gap shorter than a lower probability limit from the Phase I geometric fit (C9, C10); evaluated on the day each E-I report occurs |
| M7 | Count chart (frequency only) | daily count $N_t$ | $N_t$ above the NB upper quantile from Phase I (C5, C7: overdispersion-aware) |

### M.1 As-shipped replica
A per-day engine call costs about 12 ms (measured 13 Oct), so 18 scenarios × 1,000 replicates × 365-1,095 days would take about a day. Step 5 therefore implements `study/sim/shipped.py`, a vectorised replica of `calculate_risk_kinetics` + `classify` for the "re-run every day" use, including the quirks (calendar ends at the last report, issue 8; NaN z when SD = 0, issue 12). **Gate:** a test asserts that the replica's daily status equals the real engine's on 30 seeded streams (all days, both k, μ₀ ∈ {0.5, 2}). If parity fails, step 5 stops and the protocol is revised; the engine itself is never changed.

### M.2 Calibration of M2-M7 (fair comparison)
Each comparator's threshold (L, h, or quantile) is set **per replicate from its own Phase I data**, by simulating in-control continuations from the Phase I fitted model (parametric bootstrap, 200 continuations) and choosing the threshold that gives ARL₀ = 365. This is the honest, deployable version: estimation error in the baseline (C12) is part of what is measured. A **known-parameter ("oracle")** calibration, with thresholds set once per scenario from the true DGM, is reported as a sensitivity analysis. If per-replicate bootstrap calibration exceeds the step 5 time budget (§P.3), fall back to the oracle calibration as primary, and say so in §9 and in the paper.

### M.3 Weight schemes (Aim 5)
Run M1a, M2, M4, M5 under four schemes for scenarios IC5 (core), S2, SEV, REP:
- **squared** (shipped): A-I = 1, 4, 9, …, 81;
- **linear:** 1-9;
- **exponential:** 2^(i-1), i.e. 1 … 256;
- **harm-only:** 0 for A-D, squared for E-I (25, 36, 49, 64, 81).

## P. Performance measures, Monte Carlo error and run size

### P.1 Measures
For each estimand in §E, report the estimate and its **Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE)** (Morris 2019, Table 6):
- proportions (FA365, POD14, POD30): $\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$;
- CED: SD of delays / $\sqrt{n_{\text{detected}}}$;
- ARL₀: estimated as total monitored days at risk / number of first alarms (exponential/geometric MLE, handles censoring at 1,095 days); MCSE by the delta method; the Kaplan-Meier median run length is reported alongside.

### P.2 Replicates
**n = 1,000 per scenario**; **2,000 for IC5** (core in-control). Worst-case MCSE: proportion ≤ 0.016 (n = 1,000, p = 0.5); for a CED with SD ≈ 20 days, MCSE ≈ 0.7 days. Comparisons between methods use common random numbers, so paired differences have smaller MCSE.

### P.3 Seeds, code and budget
- Seeds: `numpy.random.SeedSequence(20261013)`; one child per scenario (in table order), one grandchild per replicate. Stored with the results.
- Code (step 5): `study/sim/dgm.py` (generator), `study/sim/shipped.py` (M1 replica), `study/sim/charts.py` (M2-M7 + calibration), `study/sim/run.py` (CLI, writes `study/results/*.csv`), `study/sim/report.py` (tables, figures). Tests in `study/tests/`: generator moments (mean, variance, severity mix within 3 MCSE), replica parity (M.1 gate), each chart against a hand-computed case, and seeds reproduce identical CSVs.
- Runs with the study virtual environment (it has `risk_kinematics` installed); no network.
- Budget: step 5 is two sessions (PLAN). Session 1: code + tests + a 100-replicate pilot of all scenarios (check timing and MCSE). Session 2: full run, tables, figures. If the full run is projected to exceed 2 hours of CPU, reduce n to 500 for non-core scenarios and report the larger MCSE.

### P.4 Outputs (step 5)
- **Table 1** in-control: ARL₀, FA365, alarm-days and episodes per ward-year, by method × IC1-IC9 (M1a/M1b show the miscalibration; M2-M7 should sit near 365 by design, and the gap shows baseline-estimation error).
- **Table 2** out-of-control: CED, POD14, POD30 by method × scenario; REP shown separately as "false harm signals".

- **Figure 1** ARL₀ by method across μ₀ and θ (log scale, reference line at 365). **Figure 2** POD30 by method and scenario with MCSE bars. **Figure 3** one illustrative synthetic stream with each method's alarms marked.

## 7. Pre-registered expectations (stated now, checked in step 5)
1. M1a ARL₀ far below 365 days (the step 3 probe suggests OUTSIDE on about 9% of days), and lower still for any alert.
2. M4 and M5 detect S15 and R60 sooner than M3 and M2 at equal ARL₀ (small sustained shifts favour EWMA/CUSUM).
3. Every score-based method (M1-M5) signals in REP at a rate close to its detection rate for a comparable rise in score: the score cannot tell reporting from harm.
4. M7 does not detect SEV; M6 and the harm-only weights do.
5. At μ₀ = 0.5, all methods have long CEDs; M6 is relatively strongest.
These are hypotheses, not results; the paper reports whatever step 5 finds.

## 8. Limits of the design (for the Discussion)
- No day-of-week or seasonal pattern, no exposure variation, no reporting backlog (late-entered reports), no clustering across wards. Each would increase false alarms in practice; the in-control results are therefore a best case.
- Severity levels are drawn independently of each other and of the count.
- One ward at a time; the multi-ward false-alarm burden is estimated as (wards) × (alarms per ward-year), not simulated.
- The as-shipped arm assumes the user checks the status daily and the date filter covers all data since the start of the stream.

## 9. Deviations log and code choices

Logged in step 5 session 2 (21 Oct 2026), after the 100-replicate pilot and before the full run. The pilot had shown the four issues below. Choices were made from pilot findings and design reasoning only; no full-run result was looked at first.

**Code choices (session 1, kept):** REP and SEV draw the total daily count once (NB, post-change total mean) and then the level mix, so per-level means match section D. Charts are not reset after an alarm. Day without a new report repeats the previous M1 status. ARL pool: 200 continuations x 1,000 days, ARL0 = days at risk / alarms, threshold = smallest value with pool ARL0 >= 365.

**(a) As-shipped M1a ARL0 about 19 days (OUTSIDE) and about 9 days (any alert).** No change. This is the Aim 1 result and is reported as it is (pre-registered expectation 1).

**(b) Calibration of M2-M7: oracle becomes primary, bootstrap becomes a sensitivity analysis.** Reason: the per-replicate bootstrap lands above 365 (pilot: 600-700 at mu0 = 2, 1,400-2,200 at mu0 = 0.5), because of estimation error in the Phase I fit and because ARL is convex in the threshold. That mixes calibration error into every method equally and hides the differences in detection that Aim 2 compares at a common ARL0. Oracle calibration (thresholds set once per scenario and weight scheme from the true model, pool of 1,000 continuations x 1,000 days, seed spawn key (scenario, 10^9)) puts the methods on the same ARL0 by design. Both runs use the same streams. Protocol section M.2 already named the oracle run as the sensitivity analysis and the fall-back; here the roles swap. The bootstrap run is kept in `study/results/full/bootstrap_sensitivity/` (Table S1) and is the "deployable" estimate that includes baseline-estimation error (C12). Note: with discrete scores the smallest threshold that reaches ARL0 >= 365 can give a longer ARL0 (visible for M7 and for M2/M3 at mu0 = 0.5); this is reported, not corrected.

**(c) M6 g-chart dropped from the compared set.** Reason: a lower-limit g-chart needs rare events. E-I reports are about 15% of reports and, at mu0 = 2, occur on about 30% of days. At day resolution no integer limit gives ARL0 365 (the pilot shows the chart never alarms at mu0 = 2 and 8). The alternative "reports between E-I events" has the same flaw: with P(E-I) = 0.15 per report, a gap of 0 reports already has probability 0.15, far above the 1/110 per E-I event needed for ARL0 365. A usable g-chart needs a rarer event class (for example G-I, about 1%), which is a different protocol. The code and tests for M6 stay; `run.py --include-m6` runs it. Consequence: pre-registered expectation 4 (M6 detects SEV) cannot be tested; the harm-only weight scheme (Table 3) is the severity-aware comparator instead.

**(d) M2 and M3 coincide.** Both are a threshold on x_t calibrated to the same ARL0, so they differ only by how the threshold is picked (M2: pooled (1 - 1/365) quantile; M3: smallest value with pooled ARL0 >= 365). Both rows are kept in the tables, with the note that they are the same rule. The paper should treat them as one method (revised/Shewhart) and not claim agreement between two independent charts.

**Other additions.** (i) `run.py --workers N` runs one (scenario, weight scheme) cell per process; CSVs are identical to the serial run (test). (ii) `nb_upper_quantile` returns the Poisson quantile for theta = infinity (bug found when oracle calibration passed theta = inf to M7: IC3, IC6, IC9 M7 ARL0 was 1-3 days). (iii) Figures are drawn with Pillow: matplotlib is not installed in the study venv.
